Re: Explanation of Doohickey/Balancer mechanism function/pro


I'll take a shot, but someone more experienced is welcome to correct me.


There is a chain that drives the balancers. These balancers are designed to offset some of the vibration from the one big piston. As all things mechanical wear, the chain will wear and stretch a little bit. This is where the ilder shaft lever and spring (doohickey) come into play. The spring will pull to a set amount of tension. You tighten the screw on the lever to hold it in place. Every so often (recommended with oil changes) you loosen this screw, the spring pulls, and the sprocket will adjust to take up any slack in the chain. If you ever replaced or adjusted a timing belt on a car, the mechanism is similar enough to get the concept.

Re: Explanation of Doohickey/Balancer mechanism function/pro


Yes, all chains stretch, and in addition to the wear on the chain, the sprockets wear too. And just as your drive components need replacement, eventually the inner ones will too. Obviously, if properly maintained, this is far down the road for the balancer in comparison to the drive system.

If NOT properly maintained, the balancer system's chain will start slapping around and wear more quickly. Not good.

Sometimes you'll hear a "click" when adjusting the doo. Recommended adjustment is every other oil change (unscrew 360 degrees to loosen and then retorque to 69 in/lbs).


Do the torsion spring. Theres no good reason not to if you're already digging into the engine there. I've never seen any slack taken up on my system after 15k miles with the torsion spring. Chances are you wont have to look back for 10's of thousands of miles.


Good upgrade. Required upgrade. If your KLR was bought new (first gen, right?) yours is most likely broken and needs replacement.

My 2008 has 96,000 miles. I haven't ridden it much since returning from the TAT last summer because I was hearing some noises from the balancer chain area at the end of that trip. I have another KLR to ride.


I had put an Eagle Doohickey and torsion spring in at around 20,000 miles mainly for the torsion spring. At around 80,000 miles I found the adjustment bolt loose - my fault - and the spring broken. The slack chain had worn five teeth - not all - on one sprocket to very sharp points. I replaced it.


I pulled the left cover yesterday just to check the components. All the driven sprockets look good. The chain is tight and the doo looks like it has another 20,000 miles of adjustment range left.


The teeth on the drive sprocket on the crank shaft are looking well worn. Some of the nonmetallic coating on the chain guide under the crank shaft sprocket is worn down to metal and I am hoping that is where the noise is coming from. Ordered a new one.


I think it is good for another 20,000 miles and then will serve better as a parts bike. With out the loose spring problem, I think it would have been good up to 150,000 miles.

"Chain Stretch" is a misnomer.


Chains don't stretch.


They wear over time, which creates slack in the chain's original measured length.
