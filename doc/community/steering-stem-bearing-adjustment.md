Re: Steering Stem Bearing Adjustment/Health


"Resistance in spots" usually is an indicator of a bad bearing. The bars turn a little back and forth instead of turning round and round, and the rollers wear grooves into the races from operating within a narrow range. If you've had to readjust them that often, my guess is that they are probably shot.

Re: Steering Stem Bearing Adjustment/Health


Are you loosening the lower fork clamps when you adjust the steering head bearings?


The upper and lower triples are held a set distance apart by the fork tubes. If the tubes are not free to slide in the clamp (changing the distance) the bearing adjustment is bending the triples and the tightening you feel is false.


Another cause of repeated adjustments is a bearing race that is not fully seated on installation. It keeps moving closer to being properly seated and causes loss of preload. Probably not the case here since you didn't replace the races but still one cause of loose adjustment.


Best,


Jeff

Big_rider,


Maybe when you get in there again try removing the wheel, forks and upper triple. All you need to get the bearing preload correct is the lower triple with stem.


Adjust it so it has no play and a smidge. I just do it by feel. Once the adjustment is to your liking install the "claw" washer, upper triple and top fastener. Just snug the top nut. Install the forks and tighten the upper triple clamping bolts. Then tighten the upper stem nut which holds the upper triple and "claw" washer in place. Get that much done and then install the wheel and handlebars.


Finally put some weight on the front end and bounce it a few times. Then tighten the lower triple fork tube clamping bolts.


Now take a test ride and see how it goes.

I had an old Honda XL600 that after 60,000 miles had marks in the bearing races - but only at 6:00 and 12:00 of the bearing race of course. On the KLR I see it's not easy to get the races out, so I've tightened mine a few times over it's 95,000 miles... I'm thinking I'm getting close to buying fresh bearings and races, grabbing the TIG and popping in new stuff.

Re: Steering Stem Bearing Adjustment/Health


When I remove them I make a diagonal cut with a dremel tool with a cut off wheel almost through the race. Then I whack it in the cut with a cold chisel and medium size hammer. The race is very brittle and the whack with the chisel cracks it. Then it will slide right off.


For installation I freeze the lower triple tree and steering stem. I lube the bearing, hang it from a bent wire and heat it with a hot air gun. When the bearing is pretty hot I get the frozen steering stem and quickly slide the bearing in place. I have a piece of pipe available to slip over the stem and down on the inner race. I tap it with a hammer to make sure it is fully seated.


Freezing the outer race and then tapping it in place using the race you removed from the steering head works well. Make sure you either have the race you are using as a driver shrunk or cut so it fits into the steering head and you can pull it back out. I got one stuck in the steering head once and it was interesting for a few minutes while I figured out how to remove it without damaging the new race. Make sure the steering head recess is very clean so the race can fully seat. If you don't get the race fully seated you will continue to have adjustment issues.


Best,


Jeff
