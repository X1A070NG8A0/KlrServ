<https://www.klr650.net/forums/forum/klr650-forums-klr650-net/klr650-maintenance-klr-wrenching-modifications-technical-support/95400-doohickey-wtf-is-a-doohickey?p=1575847#post1575847>

Re: Doohickey? WTF is a doohickey?


The big bore single makes a lot of vibration, and to smooth this out, the engine is designed with a pair of balancer shafts. The counter-weights on these shafts help offset the vibrations of the single big piston. These shafts are chain driven off a sprocket on the crankshaft.


Because the chain can stretch over the life of the bike, there is a tensioner mechanism. This tensioner mechanism is locked into place with a small lever -the balancer lever, aka doohickey.


Issues:

The OEM doohickey is not as rugged as it should be, and this can lead to a broken lever. Broken levers have caused dramatic engine failures. Earlier bikes had a much weaker lever (pre 2008).


The OEM springs are typically too long, leading to a too loose balancer chain. Too loose chains cause excessive wear on the chain and potential chain failure.


There is an excellent replacement lever and spring set from a vendor on this forum. Changing out the OEM parts for the Eager Mike parts is highly recommended for better reliability and peace of mind.


Search tool will find you dozens of stories. Note, some riders claim its really a myth - those riders have never held a broken doohickeys nor seen a damaged/destroyed engine.

Re: Doohickey? WTF is a doohickey?


05 levers can and do break. The 05 spring is the most common failure. Don't forget that even functional parts are sometimes crimped so hard into the engine cover that the system cannot adjust properly. The 08+ lever is much stronger and I haven't heard of one breaking, but the fit is still sub-par and the spring is too long and has been reported to lose tension quickly.


While this does qualify as a bit of a KLR Kool-Aid, Doing "the Doo" is very cheap insurance against a significant breakdown, that while covered under warrantee - will keep the rider off the bike for weeks if not months. There is no engine program for the KLR. Dealers service departments must order the parts they need to rebuild the original motor - that takes time.

Jeffx - Welcome - Post #1 I see. Good place to dive in.

<Edit> Brian got it right, and beat me posting up. Here's a bit more information supporting what he says....<edit>

To answer your question, it's a Yes and no. The actual lever or Quadrant is stronger in the Post 07 bikes- but does not fit on the balancer idler shaft with the same precision of Eager Mike's. What this means is that the idler shaft can still rock within the quadrant. The possibility of broken parts circulating in the engine have been reduced with the redesign, but Mike's parts still make the bike better, and ensure the system works as well as the design allows.

Then there is the spring. The pre 08 spring had some sort of mfg process defect which cause one of the eyes to fail. The broken spring could then be collected by the balancer or cam chain and really reak some havok internally. The new 08+ spring does not have this fault, but it's now too long and doesn't actually tension the system properly. While it may not break and take a trip though the cam chain, it's still not doing the job it is supposed to. The answer is to install a torsion spring. It replaces the oem spring design with a more updated design that provides consistent pressure over the life of the balancer chain.

The combination of these faults on the gen 1 bikes was bad enough, however for Kawi to screw up the redesign this bad is really something to blame on the lawyers and bean counters - there is no way the engineers won the internal debate. They've SEEN Mikes parts and were not able to implement.

So where does that leave the new KLR owner? instead of getting upset - or waiting for it to fail under warrantee. It is much wiser to simply fix it yourself and enjoy the bike. Check Eager Mike's product in the Vendor section.

In addition to the wealth of information here, you can also check the KLR Faqs at Marknet to get an overload of KLR knowledge: http://www.klr650.marknet.us/

If you are shoping for a KLR, the most imporant thing I've learned here and from my own experience is to not wait too long. Chose a Bike and get to riding. IF you have problems we can help, but this does not mean you will have problems, or if you do that you will have all the problems you read about. I've had minimal issues other than a broken Doo spring. Going on 8 years and 30K miles. My problem is finding time to ride lately, before that it was only tires, gas, and oil.

The KLR is a vibrating machine, as are all singles. The balancer shafts and all simply tame the beast, and make it a daily driver. You will lose nuts and bolts eventually, but can avoid this with the liberal use of Blue Loctite when doing maintenance.

We don't see as many of the E model broken, but they still break. I'm not sure why, as they are so long they have little tension.
