Re: radiator/coolant issues


Since you are going to have to remove the radiator any way, I would first remove the fan so that I could test it and see both sides. I would also drain some of the fluid into a cup down at the water pump and have the freezing temperature tested at an auto parts store.


Then I would fill it with a proper water antifreeze mix and run it looking for wet spots to show. A radiator shop will likely test it themselves, but I would want to know for myself where the leak(s) is. If you have a regulated air source that you can turn down to about 15 psi, you can plug one hoses and blow air in the other with the cap on and the radiator under water and look for bubbles. Don't worry about bubbles from the nipple on the side of the neck because that is where the cap relieves high pressure to.


You should test your reservoir also because it may have frozen and cracked.


As Tumbleweed says, a good radiator shop should be able to fix it for you. I have had two repaired. Make sure you find a real radiator shop that actually repairs radiators rather than a general repair shop that includes radiators in their advertising. They just take it to a radiator shop. I like to take the radiator in to the guy who will do the work and talk with him about it.
