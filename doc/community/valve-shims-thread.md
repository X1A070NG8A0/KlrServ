Re: Valve Shims


I recommend opening the bike to inspect the clearance, pull ALL shims and record the current shim sizes. Then button it up and order the shims you need PLUS the next size down.


You may not need to order that many shims actually. Often the shims are different and the stagger in size is usually one or two steps apart so you can reutilize the shims to make your adjustments. As the smallest shim needs to be replace, it can be moved to one of the larger shim valves to replace it. Clear as mud?


My bike has 2 .250 shims and 2 .255 in it. When I replace the .250 with a .245, the .250 will be needed eventually in the .255 slot. So my next order would be 2 .245 and 2 .240 then I'll have the shims I need onhand for the next adjustment (if needed).


The valves stabilize after the first couple of adjustments. 12K miles or so for me, no adjustment required at 18K. I'm going in to inspect again soon, I just turned 25K.


The problem with the shim kits that are readily available is that they have a bunch of larger shims we seldom if ever need. Unless you are doing Techdays and want to carry a full selection, it's less $$ to figure out where your engine is and get the shims for it specifically.


Just my opinion of course.


As for sources, I have only ordered shims from Fred at Arrohead in Moab. He's increased prices a little, and on the the banned list appearantly so I can't give you a direct link. I'm shopping the regular supply sources, bikebandit, RonAyers, etc to see who all is competing. The deal with Fred was he has 'em, knows what he's selling, and ships fast. Tough combo to beat and he'll probably still be getting my business.
